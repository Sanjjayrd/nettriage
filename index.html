How to Think Like an Analyst

Core SOC Methodology
"An anomaly is a starting point for investigation, not proof of compromise. Detection tells an analyst where to look. Investigation determines what actually happened."
01

Observe Unusual Behaviour

Notice deviations in packet counts, host fan-out, protocol usage, or timing bursts.

02

Identify Source & Destination

Determine host roles: Is this a workstation, a domain controller, a default gateway, or external?

03

Determine Involved Protocol

Examine the protocol: Is it expected for this host role? Is it plaintext (HTTP) or encrypted (TLS)?

04

Establish if Activity is Expected

Check business context: Scheduled backups, IT vulnerability scans, or software updates often mimic attacks.

05

Look for Supporting Indicators

Correlate: Did the scanning host follow up with authenticated SMB or SSH logins? Are errors generated?

06

Correlate with Other Telemetry

Consult endpoint logs (EDR/Sysmon), firewall logs, and authentication servers to complete the picture.

07

Decide Action & Document

Conclude: Mark as benign expected activity, tune monitoring rules, or escalate for incident containment.

Wireshark Capture & Export Guide

Step-by-Step
Step 1

Open Wireshark

Launch Wireshark on your system and choose your active network interface (Ethernet or Wi-Fi).

Step 2

Capture Authorised Traffic

Start the capture. Only monitor traffic from devices and networks you own or have explicit authorisation to test.

Step 3

Stop the Capture

Click the red square button to stop recording once you have captured the target activity.

Step 4

Export as CSV

In Wireshark's menu, click:
File → Export Packet Dissections → As CSV...

Step 5

Upload to NetTriage

Drag the exported .csv file into the upload zone above to view your triage report!
